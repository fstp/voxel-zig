# 0025 Terrain surface rules: snow, mud and shore wetness

- Status: implemented (10.8, 2026-11-27)
- Depends on: ADR 0019 (procedural materials), ADR 0022 (DC world), ADR 0023 (sea)
- Task: Vikunja #159 (#81), "10.8 Terrain surface rules: snow, mud and shore wetness"

## Context

The DC world spans 49-73 m with a sea at 55 m. The terrain shader already carries a
per-vertex material id (ground / rock / scree), a palette colour, two octaves of value noise
and a procedural bump. The next visual step after the foliage work was to make altitude and
water proximity visible in the surface itself: snow on the high ground, mud and a wet rim
around the sea.

The JCGT paper by Papavasiliou (2015) is a generator for small discrete objects (grass, gravel,
rocks, twigs) via tessellation and geometry shaders. Snow and mud are continuous surface layers,
not small objects: approximating a blanket with thousands of tessellated instances would be
wasteful, would z-fight with the terrain, and would still need a mask to decide where it lies.
The right tool is a fragment-shader material rule, which also fits the no-TAA/no-MSAA constraint
because it needs no blending, sorting or coverage.

## Decision

All of 10.8 lives in the terrain fragment shader in `src/gfx/dc_renderer.zig`; no new geometry,
buffers or draw calls.

New uniforms, set from `dc_main.zig`:

| Uniform | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `u_surfaces` | 1.0, `VOXEL_SURFACES=0` for A/B | 0 skips all surface rules |
| `u_sea_level` | `dcw.SEA_LEVEL` (55.0) | waterline for mud and wetness |
| `u_snow_line` | 68.0 | snow start before the noise warp |
| `u_mud_band` | 1.4 | half-width of the shore band, in metres of height |

Both masks are evaluated in the same coherent uniform branch and are noise-warped so their
edges do not follow a height contour. Both are gated on the interpolated geometric normal so
cliffs stay bare. The shader reuses one broad `noised()` sample for the warp and one finer
sample for the snow break-up and drift normal. `ndl` and the hemisphere ambient are now computed
**after** the snow normal is perturbed; before this change the snow would have been lit with the
unperturbed terrain normal and looked like flat white paint.

### Snow

```
snow_h = u_snow_line + (m.x - 0.5) * 2.0;          // 67.0-69.0 m
snow   = smoothstep(snow_h, snow_h + 3.0, height)  // full by 70-72 m
       * smoothstep(0.65, 0.85, geometric_up);     // steep faces stay bare
snow  *= 1.0 - 0.55 * smoothstep(0.35, 0.60, d.x); // bare rock patches
```

- Colour `vec3(0.84, 0.88, 0.95) * (0.90 + 0.12 * mott)`, exposure raised from 0.16 to 0.34,
  a 0.05 specular term.
- The normal is perturbed by the fine and broad noise gradients (`0.9` and `0.3`) and then
  flattened 10 % toward up, so the cap has drift shading instead of one flat white slab.
- Treeline and grass cap were lowered from 71/74 m to **66 m** in `world/scatter.zig`, below the
  snow line, so blades and conifers do not poke through the cap.

### Mud and wetness

```
shore = abs(height - u_sea_level) + (m.x - 0.5) * 1.5;   // +/-0.75 m warp
mud   = (1 - smoothstep(0, u_mud_band, shore))
      * smoothstep(0.60, 0.82, geometric_up);
wet   = (1 - smoothstep(0, 0.9, height - sea)) * step(sea - 0.05, height);
```

- Mud colour `vec3(0.34, 0.27, 0.20)`; the lowest 0.9 m above water mixes a further 55 % toward
  `vec3(0.16, 0.13, 0.10)` and gets a 0.30 specular term, so the waterline reads wet.
- The mask is symmetric in height, so the shallow lake bed darkens as well; the transparent water
  shader then has a darker bed under it.
- Exposure is scaled by 0.92 where mud lies, keeping it a dark band rather than a bright beach.

## Why not the JCGT approach here

Papavasiliou's paper is about **small discrete objects** whose geometry can be generated by
tessellation and geometry shaders: grass, gravel, rocks, fallen leaves, twigs. Its useful ideas
for us are the terrain-derived distribution (no extra density maps) and continuous LOD; both
belong in the 10.9 scatter work, implemented with our existing instancing. Porting the
tessellation/geometry-shader pipeline would couple the vegetation to the terrain triangulation,
which is a dual-contouring mesh rather than the paper's ROAM heightfield, and would not help a
continuous snow or mud layer.

## Limitations

- **Height is only a proxy for distance to water.** On a gentle shore the mud band is wide; on a
  cliff it is narrow. A true distance field (jump flooding or a precomputed SDF) would be the next
  step if the shore needs to be exact.
- **No exposure or AO term.** Snow lies on every up-facing surface above the line; there is no
  shadow-map or sky-occlusion pass for it. The paper's `inclination x exposure` model is the
  reference for a future pass.
- **The terrain is terraced.** Snow on the flat tops exposes the 1 m steps that grass otherwise
  hides; the bare-rock modulation and drift normals help, but the cap still reads blocky at close
  range. This is a property of the DC terrain, not the mask.
- **No snow on trees, rocks or props**, and no displacement/drift geometry. 10.9 adds the
  discrete detail where it matters.
- **The wet specular is subtle** at a high sun angle; it is a 0.30 lobe, not a reflection.

## Follow-up

- **10.9 Scatter detail**: snow clumps/ice shards on high flat ground and mud pebbles/reeds in the
  shore band, using the same masks and the existing deterministic scatter. This is where the
  JCGT distribution idea is actually used.
- Optional later: curvature/AO exposure for snow, a true shore distance field, and the GPU Gems 2
  card/dissolve LOD for grass.
