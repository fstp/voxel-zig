# 0011 - Physically based shading, an HDR target and a filmic tone curve

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-26
- Decides for: 4.1 (HDR, exposure, tone mapping) and 4.2 (metallic-roughness BRDF)
- Depends on: 0008 (linear pipeline), 0009 (sun ramp), 0010 (clouds)

## The two problems

"Still too bright" and "flat" are the same defect seen twice: the scene was shaded
straight into an sRGB framebuffer, so there was nowhere for light above 1.0 to go
and no exposure control at all. Every surface got `albedo * (ambient + sun*N.L)`,
which is a matte-diffuse-only response, and the whole frame sat in the top third of
the tonal range with no deep end.

## Decision

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Transport | render linear light into `RGBA16F`, resolve to the window in one fullscreen pass |
| Tone curve | ACES fitted (Narkowicz), `clamp` on the low end, filmic shoulder on the high end |
| Exposure | scalar before the curve, default 0.62, `-`/`=` (x0.92 / x1.08), `VOXEL_EXPOSURE` pins it |
| BRDF | Cook-Torrance: GGX distribution, height-correlated Smith visibility, Schlick Fresnel |
| Materials | metallic-roughness per **texture layer**, `u_material[8]` as `vec2(roughness, metallic)` |
| Environment | the sky gradient stands in for an IBL cube: `reflect(-V, N)` picks the hemisphere colour, `(1-roughness)` decides how much survives |
| Diffuse | energy conserving: `(1 - F) * (1 - metallic) * albedo / PI` |
| A/B | F11 toggles the curve, F4 keeps the old face-table path for comparison |

`u_sun_colour` is multiplied by PI inside the shader: the uniform was tuned for the
old `albedo * light` expression and is shared with the sky and cloud ramps, so
scaling it in place of retuning the sky is the cheap correct move.

## Why per layer, not per block

The mesher writes a texture-layer byte per vertex already. A uniform array indexed
by that byte gives every material in the world without touching the 24-byte vertex,
the mesher, or the chunk format. `block.layer_materials` sits next to
`texture_paths` and a test asserts the two arrays are the same length and that
every roughness is in `(0, 1]`.

## Measured

Same camera pose, same sun (az 150, el 25), before and after, terrain region only:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| mean luminance | 145.2 | **114.3** |
| median | 133.1 | 110.6 |
| p95 | 196.1 | 184.3 |
| pixels below 30 (deep shade) | 0.00 % | **3.07 %** |
| pixels at or above 250 | 0.00 % | 0.00 % |

The frame was never *clipping*; it was flat in the upper range and had no dark end
at all. The 21 percent drop in mean and the appearance of 3 percent genuinely dark
pixels is the change that reads as "less bright".

Cost: the resolve is 0.012-0.014 ms at 1280x720 (GPU query, in the HUD as `POST`).
120 fps unchanged, worst frame 8.78 ms. 170 tests pass.

## Rejected

- **Exposure alone, no float target.** Cheaper by one pass, but every value above
  1.0 is already clipped before the multiply, so raising exposure to inspect a
  highlight shows flat colour instead of detail. The float target is what makes the
  exposure control meaningful.
- **Reinhard tone mapping.** Flatter highlights and a washed midtone on a scene this
  saturated; ACES costs two extra multiply-adds.
- **Add a specular term to the existing Lambert shader without the BRDF.** This is
  what most "add shiny highlights" changes do, and it produces a highlight that does
  not move correctly with roughness or view angle, which is worse than none.

## Consequences and limits

- Everything before the resolve is linear; a shader that writes a colour by hand
  (the F6 normal debug view) still needs its own encode, and now also passes through
  the tone curve, so debug views are approximate by design.
- The environment specular is a two-colour gradient, not a cube map: no sharp
  reflections, no parallax. Water at grazing angles does read as sky, because
  Fresnel goes to 1 there, which is the one place it matters most.
- There are no normal maps or roughness maps. On 16x16 textures a PBR material
  differs from Lambert mainly in the specular lobe and in how the sky tints
  shadowed sides; the big visible gain in this change came from exposure and the
  tone curve, and it is worth saying so plainly.
- The `RGBA16F` target costs 7 MB at 1280x720 and is reallocated on resize.

## Amendment: eye adaptation for night, and a measured miss on water

Acceptance (4.3) across the cycle, measured on the same ground-level pose, terrain
region, exposure base 0.62:

| Time | mean | p5 | p95 | clipped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dawn (el 7.8) | 25.0 | 6.6 | 119.6 | 0.00 % |
| noon (el 62) | 89.6 | 11.7 | 177.7 | 0.00 % |
| dusk (el 7.8) | 16.7 | 3.2 | 119.4 | 0.00 % |
| night (el -62), before adaptation | 6.7 | 1.0 | **4.1** | 0.00 % |

Night was the failure: p95 of 4/255 is black in practice. The exposure control and
the ACES toe both act on the low end, and a moonless night has very little light to
start with. The fix is eye adaptation, not a brighter night sky:

    exposure = exposure_base * (1 + 5 * (1 - daylight()))

so night gets 6x the exposure and daylight is untouched (`daylight()` reaches 1.0 at
-6 degrees, so dawn and dusk keep their look). Night after adaptation: mean 23.4,
p50 19.5, p95 32.2, no clipping - dark blue with readable silhouettes.

Frame rate across the sweep (55 one-second samples, no recorder attached): mean
120.1 fps, min 120.0, max 123.2, worst frame 10.29 ms.

### Water at grazing angles: not met

Acceptance asked for a sky reflection at grazing angles. Measured on a sea view,
mean luminance of water pixels by screen band (20 degrees view down to about 2):

| band | mean luminance | b - r |
|---|---|---|
| near, about 20 degrees | 87.7 | 140.3 |
| mid | 89.3 | 141.0 |
| far, about 2 degrees | 92.4 | 142.2 |

A 5 percent rise across that range is flat. The reason is Schlick: `(1 - N.V)^5`
only rises within about a degree of the horizon, which is the band the fog already
covers, and the water's own bright albedo dominates everything else. Fixing it means
a real environment probe or a planar reflection - a separate feature, not a tuning
value. Recorded as 4.4 rather than papered over with a bigger F0, which would make
the water read as chrome.
